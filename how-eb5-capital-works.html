How EB-5 Capital Works

EB-5 investments are unique, and sit at the intersection of immigration law and private investment. To work properly, an EB-5 investment must connect three parts:

The FundReceives investor money
The ProjectSpends the capital
The MechanismsTrack and prove job creation

The Main Parties

Investor

Immigrant Investor

Contributes qualifying capital, documents a lawful source and path of funds, and files the immigration forms (I-526E and I-829, sometimes including I-485). Two of the most essential EB-5 requirements are that the investment must remain at risk and create at least ten qualifying U.S. jobs.

Sponsor

EB-5 Regional Center

In the indirect EB-5 route, the regional center sponsors projects and allows indirect and induced jobs to count through accepted economic models (RIMS II, IMPLAN). Under the current RIA framework, regional centers also carry more compliance responsibility, including project filings and promoter oversight.

NCE

New Commercial Enterprise

The fund entity that receives investor money and re-routes it to the project, usually as a loan or preferred equity.

JCE

Job-Creating Entity

The project company that actually spends capital on qualifying activities. Its documented spending is what proves job creation, an essential requirement for EB-5 investors.

Oversight

Independent Professionals

Fund administrators, economists, construction monitors, auditors, and counsel help verify project structure and keep the process compliant.

How Capital Moves

  1. Subscription & EscrowThe investor signs subscription documents, followed by a wire into escrow.
  2. Release to the ProjectAfter escrow conditions are met, the NCE sends money to the project.
  3. DeploymentThe project uses the capital for construction, operating costs, or other approved uses.
  4. Tracking & VerificationA third-party fund administrator tracks spending. Monthly draw packages, inspections, lien waivers, and cost reports create the record needed later for I-829 support.
  5. RepaymentAfter the sustainment period and project milestones are complete, the project repays or redeems the capital to its EB-5 investors.

Documentation from Each Party

EB-5 Regional Center

  • I-956F filingUSCIS form that establishes the project's eligibility before investor petitions can be filed
  • Business plan, economic report, budget, schedule, and TEA or rural supportOften included in the I-956F filing
  • Promoter disclosures and oversight records
  • Fund administration, AML/KYC controls, and a process for material changes

NCE

  • PPM, operating agreement, subscription agreement, and risk disclosures
  • Loan documents or preferred equity documents
  • Escrow, fund administration, banking resolutions, and redeployment policy
  • Investor notices, capital account records, and distribution statements

JCE

  • Construction contracts, permits, budget, and schedule
  • Invoices, draw requests, inspections, lien waivers, and payment proofs

Investor

  • Source-of-funds records, including tax returns, payroll records, sale documents, loan files, and bank statements
  • Immigration filings such as I-526E, I-485, and later I-829
  • Identity and status records, including passports, visas, and civil documents

Common Capital Structures

Broadly, EB-5 projects use one of two main structures.

Loan Model

The fund lends money to the project. The return is usually a fixed coupon, and the project must follow the loan terms and covenants.

Preferred Equity Model

The fund buys a preferred class in the project or holding entity. Returns depend on the agreement and available cash flow.

In practice, both structures can work well for EB-5 investors, and the availability of each often depends on project design and investor needs. The better structure is the one with stronger governance, cleaner documentation, and clearer evidence. Many offerings favor preferred equity because it can be more flexible for a project sponsor, while a loan can provide stronger remedies if the deal goes off track.

How Jobs Are Counted

Regional centers typically use RIMS II or IMPLAN to convert qualified project spending into job numbers. The economic impact report should identify the location, the qualifying costs, the timing, and the job cushion.

The project must then follow that model closely. Spending has to be categorized correctly and supported with records. That makes the I-829 process much easier later because the evidence already exists.

Tips to Investors

The following is a list of practices that is worth ensuring a regional center is following for a smoother EB-5 process:

  • Synchronizes funding and filings so capital is not taken before escrow terms are clear.
  • Documents spending in real time with draw packages and reports ready when needed.
  • Confirms loan maturities and project timelines cover the sustainment period.
  • Publishes a clear redeployment policy if capital may return early.
  • Trains all team members and agents to avoid unauthorized promises about timing, returns, or approvals.

Key Takeaway

EB-5 projects work when every link in the chain is traceable and professionally managed. The investor proves lawful funds, the regional center and NCE maintain a clean structure, and the JCE spends against a documented budget. When these things happen, financing can close, the project can be built, and investors can realize their immigration goals.
